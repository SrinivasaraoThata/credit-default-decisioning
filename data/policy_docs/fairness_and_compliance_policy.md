# Fairness and Compliance

Credit decisions must not use protected characteristics (sex, marital
status, age) as the primary or sole justification presented to an
applicant. Where these fields are inputs to the underlying risk model, the
explanation given to an applicant should be grounded in the behavioral and
account factors driving the score (payment history, utilization, trend),
not demographic attributes, even where a demographic attribute has some
statistical association with the outcome.

Default rate and false-positive rate should be monitored across demographic
groups on an ongoing basis. A meaningfully higher false-positive rate for
one group than another, at similar actual default rates, is a compliance
concern that warrants closer scrutiny of borderline cases in that group,
not automatic denial. This is one of the reasons borderline cases are
routed to manual review rather than decided automatically: a human reviewer
provides a check against a systematic pattern in the automated score that
would otherwise go uncorrected.

Any applicant who is denied or routed to manual review is entitled to a
plain-language explanation grounded in their own account behavior.
