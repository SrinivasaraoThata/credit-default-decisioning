# Payment History and Repayment Behavior

Repayment status over the most recent billing cycle is the single strongest
signal of default risk. An applicant who is currently delinquent (repayment
status of 1 month or more overdue) carries substantially higher risk than one
with a clean recent payment record, regardless of longer-term history.

Trend matters as much as the most recent snapshot. An applicant whose
repayment status is worsening over the last six months (moving from on-time
toward delinquent) should be treated as higher risk than an applicant with an
equally poor but stable or improving status, since a worsening trend suggests
the applicant's financial position is deteriorating rather than recovering.

A single missed payment is not automatically disqualifying. Applicants with
one isolated delayed payment in an otherwise clean six-month history, and no
worsening trend, are generally acceptable risks and should not be denied on
that basis alone.

Accounts with no recent balance or consumption (no active use of credit) are
not evidence of good repayment behavior on their own. They simply lack a
recent signal and should be evaluated on other factors, including
utilization and payment amount history.
