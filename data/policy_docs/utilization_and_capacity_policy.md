# Credit Utilization and Repayment Capacity

Average credit utilization (the share of an applicant's credit limit carried
as an outstanding bill) above 80 percent across recent months indicates
elevated strain and should raise the applicant's risk assessment, even if
payments have been made on time. High utilization paired with an already
worsening payment trend is a compounding risk factor, not two independent
ones, and should be weighed together rather than in isolation.

Utilization that is consistently low (under 30 percent) alongside on-time
payments is a strong positive signal and generally supports approval at
standard terms.

Where income is not directly available, average monthly payment amount
relative to credit limit stands in as a rough proxy for repayment capacity.
This proxy is weaker than verified income and should not, on its own, be the
deciding factor for a denial. It is one input among several, and any
decision that rests primarily on this proxy alone should be routed to
manual review rather than auto-denied, given the proxy's known limitations.
